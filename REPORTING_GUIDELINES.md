# Reporting Guidelines

## Purpose

These guidelines explain how reports of unacceptable behavior in the Vayu community should be submitted, reviewed, and handled.

They complement the [Code of Conduct](CODE%20OF%20CONDUCT.md) and are intended to provide a clear, fair, and respectful reporting process.

## What Should Be Reported

Reports may concern behavior that violates the Vayu Code of Conduct or otherwise creates an unsafe, hostile, or seriously disruptive community environment.

Examples include:

- Harassment or targeted abuse
- Threats or intimidation
- Discriminatory or hateful conduct
- Sexualized or unwelcome conduct
- Doxxing or sharing private information without permission
- Repeated disruptive behavior
- Malicious impersonation or abuse of project spaces
- Other conduct that reasonably violates the project's community standards

Technical bugs and security vulnerabilities should use the appropriate technical reporting channels instead of this process.

## How to Report

Reports should be made privately rather than through a public issue, pull request, or discussion.

When possible, include:

- What happened
- When and where it happened
- The GitHub username or other identifier of the people involved
- Links to relevant issues, pull requests, comments, commits, or discussions
- Screenshots or other supporting evidence
- Whether the behavior is ongoing
- Any immediate concerns about safety or privacy

You do not need to have complete evidence before making a report.

If you are unable to provide all of this information, submit the report with whatever details you can safely provide.

## Privacy

Reports will be handled as privately as reasonably possible.

Information from a report will only be shared with people who need it to review or respond to the situation. Reporters should avoid including unnecessary personal or sensitive information.

No reporter should be publicly identified or targeted for making a good-faith report.

## Review Process

Reports will be reviewed as promptly as reasonably possible.

The review process may include:

1. **Acknowledgement** — confirm that the report has been received when a response channel is available.
2. **Initial review** — determine whether the report falls within the scope of the Code of Conduct or these guidelines.
3. **Evidence review** — examine relevant messages, issues, pull requests, commits, or other available information.
4. **Context review** — consider the surrounding circumstances and the perspectives of the people involved.
5. **Decision** — determine an appropriate response based on the available information and severity of the conduct.
6. **Follow-up** — communicate the outcome to the reporter when appropriate and when doing so does not compromise privacy or safety.

Reports will be considered on a case-by-case basis. A report does not automatically mean that a violation occurred.

## Possible Actions

Depending on the circumstances, maintainers may take actions such as:

- Asking someone to stop or change the behavior
- Removing or editing inappropriate content
- Closing or locking a discussion, issue, or pull request
- Issuing a private warning
- Temporarily restricting participation
- Permanently restricting participation
- Removing project permissions or roles
- Reporting serious violations to GitHub or appropriate authorities when required

The response will consider the severity, frequency, context, and impact of the behavior.

## Conflicts of Interest

Anyone reviewing a report should avoid participating in a decision when they have a significant conflict of interest.

If a report concerns a maintainer or another person responsible for reviewing reports, the reporter should state that in the report so an alternative reviewer can be considered.

## Good-Faith Reporting

Reports made honestly and in good faith are welcome, even when a report ultimately does not result in enforcement action.

Knowingly false reports, fabricated evidence, or deliberate misuse of the reporting process may themselves violate the Code of Conduct.

## Retaliation

Retaliation against someone for making a good-faith report, participating in a review, or providing relevant information is not acceptable.

## Urgent Safety Concerns

The Vayu reporting process is not an emergency service.

If there is an immediate risk of harm, contact appropriate local emergency services or a trusted adult or responsible authority rather than relying on a repository report.
